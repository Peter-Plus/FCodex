# Codex shell / ChatGPT tabs experiment

Status: **NOT READY for production**.

Only the isolated .mode-prototype executable and investigations scripts were changed.
Official installation, production Fade, and startup animation were not changed by this experiment.

Implementation:
- The experimental global product atom stays Codex.
- A separate derived content atom follows the current native route.
- Native homeDraftKey scopes isolate normal drafts.
- Tabs preserve complete native route state and query parameters.
- ChatGPT temporary conversation identifiers are generated by the native helper.
- All chat rendering, models and attachments remain native.
- Account mode preferences are not updated by the experimental gqt action.

Validated:
- Real ChatGPT composer while the global product mode and work preference stay Codex/coding.
- Codex and two ChatGPT normal drafts survive repeated switching separately.
- Native ChatGPT model picker and attachment entries appear.
- Navigation into a Codex conversation and native Back/Forward follow the correct content mode.
- Temporary-chat flag is separate between tabs and restored from its native route query.

Blocker:
Temporary-chat text does not survive switching away and returning. Normal text is retained.
A control without protected draft keys confirmed that switching from ordinary to temporary chat
normally opens a separate empty draft, and toggling back restores ordinary text.
The additional failure is loss of NEW text entered in the temporary draft after tab switching.
Do not bypass this by persisting temporary chat text to disk.

Untested:
Actual sending/reply, uploads, existing ChatGPT history routing, restart restoration,
full Fade integration, startup animation integration, future official versions.
No messages were sent and no files were uploaded.

Evidence: split-verification.json.
Builder: build-mode-prototype.mjs (specific to the inspected official version).
Prototype toolbar: install-split-tabs.js.
The final verification script correctly failed on the temporary draft combination;
its normal-draft assumptions must not be relaxed to call this complete.
